# Evaluation Case: Identifying Good Investments

## User Prompt

How do I identify stocks with good returns and reasonable risk?

---

## Expected Reasoning Challenges

The system should recognize:

A good company is not always a good investment.

Investment returns depend on:

- business quality
- future growth
- valuation
- uncertainty
- risk

---

## Expected Structure

A strong answer should develop:

Expected Return

=

Business Quality

×

Growth

×

Valuation

×

Risk Management


Where:

Business Quality:

- competitive advantage
- pricing power
- customer loyalty
- operational excellence

Growth:

- market opportunity
- reinvestment opportunities
- durability

Valuation:

- price relative to future expectations

Risk:

- uncertainty
- downside scenarios
- leverage
- concentration

---

## Required Validation

The system should test:

### Missing Variables

Examples:

- management quality
- incentives
- industry disruption
- capital allocation

---

### Contradictions

Examples:

- Excellent company with terrible valuation produces poor returns.

---

### Boundary Conditions

Examples:

This framework works better for:

- long-term investing

Less applicable for:

- short-term trading
- momentum speculation
- options pricing

---

## Expected Compression

Possible:

"Good investments are not just good companies; they are good businesses purchased at attractive prices with controlled risk."

---

## Quality Criteria

Pass if:

✓ separates company quality from investment return  
✓ explains uncertainty  
✓ avoids stock picking certainty  
✓ identifies risk/reward tradeoffs  
✓ produces a repeatable process
